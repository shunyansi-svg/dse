Two radius bars — the trap this topic sets every year

The metallic radius of an atom is always LARGER than the radius of the ion it forms when it loses its outer shell. Students who compare "K atom" values with "Na⁺ ion" values get the trend exactly backwards. The bars below are drawn to the same scale.

Step 1 — Worked example: how the comparison is written

Explain why magnesium has a higher melting point than sodium. (2)

Line 1 — compare the IONS Mg forms Mg²⁺ while Na forms Na⁺. Mg²⁺ carries a higher charge (2+ against 1+), so the electrostatic attraction between the ion and the delocalised electrons is stronger. Line 2 — compare the ELECTRON COUNT Each Mg atom also releases TWO delocalised electrons while each Na atom releases only one, so there are more delocalised electrons per atom to attract. Line 3 — finish with the consequence Both effects make the metallic bonding stronger, so MORE ENERGY is needed to overcome the attraction and the melting point is higher. Rejected: "Mg has 12 electrons and Na has 11". The TOTAL number of electrons in the atom is never the answer \u2014 only the delocalised electrons count.
