Step 1 — Worked example: reacting-mass arithmetic on a salt

Everything in this topic comes back to the mole. Read the reasoning line first, then the arithmetic.

Question 2.50 g of hydrated copper(II) sulfate, CuSO₄.xH₂O, is heated to constant mass and 1.60 g of anhydrous CuSO₄ remains. Find x. (Molar masses: CuSO₄ = 160 g mol⁻¹, H₂O = 18.0 g mol⁻¹) Step 1 - find the mass that was LOST, by subtraction mass of water lost = 2.50 - 1.60 = 0.90 g The water left as a gas, so it is the mass LOST. This subtraction line is a marking point. Step 2 - convert BOTH masses to moles n(CuSO4) = 1.60 / 160 = 0.0100 mol n(H2O) = 0.90 / 18.0 = 0.0500 mol Step 3 - divide by the smaller to get a whole-number ratio x = n(H2O) / n(CuSO4) = 0.0500 / 0.0100 = 5 So the formula is CuSO₄.5H₂O. Significant figures The data are given to 3 significant figures (2.50 g, 1.60 g), so the answer is quoted to 3 s.f. where it matters, and x itself is a whole number because it is a ratio of moles. Sense check: the water is about five times the moles of the salt, so x should be about 5. If you got 0.2, you divided the wrong way round.
