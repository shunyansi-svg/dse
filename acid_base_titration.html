Examiner wording to memorise: dissolve in a beaker → transfer with a funnel and rinse the beaker and funnel into the flask → make up to the mark with deionised water (bottom of the meniscus on the mark, at eye level) → stopper and invert repeatedly. C · Percentage uncertainty of the volumetric work

% uncertainty = (uncertainty ÷ reading) × 100. A titre needs two burette readings, so its uncertainty is ±0.10 cm³.

D · Standard solution, and the ×10 aliquot trap

Solid is weighed, dissolved, made up to 250.0 cm³. Only 25.0 cm³ of it is titrated — so moles in the flask = moles in the aliquot × 10.

Step 1 — Worked example: the ratio is the mark

This is the calculation the whole practical exists to produce. Read the reasoning before the arithmetic.

Question 25.0 cm³ of dilute hydrochloric acid is titrated with 0.100 mol dm⁻³ sodium hydroxide. The mean titre is 24.40 cm³. Calculate the concentration of the acid. Equation: HCl + NaOH → NaCl + H₂O Step 1 - moles of the solution you know BOTH facts about The burette gave you the volume of NaOH, and you know its concentration, so start there. n(NaOH) = c x V = 0.100 mol dm⁻³ x (24.40 / 1000) dm³ = 0.100 x 0.02440 = 2.440 x 10⁻³ mol The divided-by-1000 line is a marking point on its own. Do it BEFORE substituting. Step 2 - the ratio, from the BALANCED equation HCl : NaOH = 1 : 1, so n(HCl) = 2.440 x 10⁻³ mol This line is the second marking point. Write it out. Step 3 - concentration of the acid The acid was pipetted into the flask: V = 25.0 cm³ = 0.0250 dm³ c(HCl) = n / V = 2.440 x 10⁻³ mol / 0.0250 dm³ = 0.0976 mol dm⁻³ Sense check The acid is slightly less concentrated than the alkali, and the titre (24.40) is slightly less than the aliquot (25.0), which is consistent. A 1:1 titration should give roughly equal volumes for roughly equal concentrations - if your answer is 1000x out, you did not convert cm³. The ratio trap: in this reaction the ratio IS 1:1. In the next task it is not, and using 1:1 there is the single most common way to lose the method mark.
