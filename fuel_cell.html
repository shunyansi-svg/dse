Fuel cell vs primary cell

  • • Advantage (MS wording): water is the only product (at the point of use) — no oxides of carbon.
  • • Produces electricity directly from the fuel — no combustion, no heat → turbine → generator steps, so higher efficiency (≈70% accepted by MS, approx).
  • • Continuous supply of fuel and oxygen from outside, so it keeps running while fed. A primary cell seals its reactants inside and runs down as they reach equilibrium.
  • • Disadvantages (MS-accepted): H₂ is often made from fossil / non-renewable resources (steam reforming); storage and transport problems; shorter life-span and cost.
  • • ❌ "Safer", "less pollution", "dangerous" are not mark-earning answers.

Hydrogen-rich fuels & carbon neutrality

Methanol fuel cell · 16.18

CH₃OH + 1½O₂ → CO₂ + 2H₂O

anode: CH₃OH + H₂O → CO₂ + 6H⁺ + 6e⁻

  • • Carbon neutral? Be honest: the cell itself releases no CO₂ — water is the only product at the point of use. The claim depends on how the hydrogen is made: from natural gas (steam reforming, CO₂ released) the "carbon-neutral" label fails; from water electrolysis using renewable electricity it becomes defensible.
  • • Atom economy of 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O = 100% — water is the only product (Oct 2025 Q6c trap: this is not the mass fraction of hydrogen in water).
  • • A fuel cell is a converter, not a store: unlike a battery it does not store charge — stop the feed and the voltage stops.

In the real lab you would observe…

  • • Bubbles at both electrodes as H₂ and O₂ are consumed; the bulb / voltmeter responds at once.
  • • Water is produced — test with anhydrous copper(II) sulfate: white → blue (the standard test). In acid mode it forms at the oxygen (cathode) side; in alkali mode at the hydrogen (anode) side.
  • • Gas volumes consumed stay in a 2 : 1 ratio (H₂ : O₂) — same ratio as electrolysis of water, run in reverse.
