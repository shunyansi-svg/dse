Syllabus Master Overview

Topic X Learning Points & Mark Scheme Secrets (Strands (a)–(c))

Every Students should learn / be able to strand of Chemical Equilibrium, with HKEAA watch-outs from mark-scheme language and Candidates’ Performance evidence 2012–2025. Single homogeneous reactions only — the exam never asks you to derive the temperature law, only to read it (NTF-2018 II-B X (c)).

Topic X (a) Dynamic equilibrium

Characteristics of dynamic equilibrium — the four-sentence definition

🎯 Key Learning Goals:
  • Describe reversible and irreversible reactions by using suitable examples: reversible — $\text{N}_{2}$ + 3H₂ ⇌ 2NH₃, esterification; irreversible (for this level) — burning magnesium, rust formation, strong acid + strong base.
  • Describe characteristics of a system existing in dynamic equilibrium: a closed system, forward and backward reactions both continuing, $r_{\text{forward}} = r_{\text{backward}} \neq 0$, and “No net change is observed” (ms bank).
  • Macroscopic constancy is the evidence, not the definition: colour intensity, gas pressure, density and all concentrations stop changing while particles keep reacting.
  • Equilibrium can be reached from either direction (start with reactants or with products) and the concentrations plateau at non-zero, generally unequal values fixed by $K_c$.
  • Read the standard graphs: concentration–time curves flattening together; rate–time curves meeting at one non-zero level; for $\text{X}_{2}$(g) ⇌ 2X(g) the product rises with twice the consumed slope (2012 Paper 1A Q27 shape).
💡 HKEAA Examiner Watch-Outs:

“At dynamic equilibrium, the rate of forward reaction is equal to the rate of backward reaction, and not equals zero” is the bank’s own defensible wording (ms bank); only about half of candidates could reproduce a correct description (CP 2016 Q10). Never write “the reaction stops” or “concentrations are equal”. A vessel that lets a gas escape never reaches equilibrium — the 2013 reflux MC hinged on “the loss of ammonia, an equilibrium state cannot be reached” (CP 2013 Q32).

Topic X (b) Equilibrium constant Kc

Kc — expression, constancy, units and ICE calculations

🎯 Key Learning Goals:
  • Express the mathematical relationship between equilibrium concentrations and $K_c$: products over reactants, each raised to its balancing coefficient — e.g. 2SO₂(g) + O₂(g) ⇌ 2SO₃(g), $K_c = \dfrac{[\text{SO}_{3}]^{2}}{[\text{SO}_{2}]^{2}[\text{O}_{2}]}$ (ms bank).
  • Recognise Kc is constant at constant temperature “irrespective of changes in concentration of reactants and products” — only a temperature change moves its value.
  • Perform calculations involving Kc: ICE tables (Initial–Change–Equilibrium) in moles, divided by the volume in dm³; find $K_c$ from equilibrium concentrations or work backwards from $K_c$; “Step marks (*) for ICE table calculations” (ms bank).
  • Derive the units from the expression: $(\text{mol dm}^{-3})^{\Delta n}$ — mol dm⁻³ for PCl₅ dissociation, mol⁻¹ dm³ for SO₃ formation, none when $\Delta n = 0$ (HI case).
  • Perform practical work on the determination of Kc: the esterification route — sealed mixture, conc. H₂SO₄ catalyst, quench and titrate (syllabus (b) “able” line).
  • Interpret magnitude: $K_c \gg 1$ products predominate; $K_c \ll 1$ reactants predominate — position language, never speed language.
💡 HKEAA Examiner Watch-Outs:

“Correct expression for Kc” scores 1 mark even if the numbers then fail (ms bank) — always write the expression first. The expression is built from equilibrium concentrations of dissolved/gaseous species only: solids and pure liquids never appear in this syllabus’s single homogeneous systems, and water counts only when it is a species in the mixture, not the solvent (NTF-2018 II-B X (c)). Units and volume: “failed to give a correct unit… wrongly regarded the total volume… as 50 cm3 instead of 50 dm3” (CP 2014 Q13). Do not confuse rate constant $k$ with equilibrium constant $K$ (CP 2012).

Topic X (c) Concentration changes

Changes in concentration — the system adjusts without changing Kc

🎯 Key Learning Goals:
  • Strand (c) verbatim: “changes in concentration result in the adjustment of the system without changing the value of Kc” — position moves, constant stays.
  • Deduce the effect of change in concentration on the position of chemical equilibrium: add a species → shift away from the addition; remove a species → shift toward it; state direction in HKEAA register — “shift to the left hand side / reactant side” (ms bank).
  • Use the reaction quotient to justify: after the stress $Q \neq K_c$; “reaction quotient > Kc, so backward reaction rate is greater than the forward reaction rate” (ms bank); the deduction with Q is explicitly expected (NTF-2018 II-B X (c)).
  • Compression = every concentration rises at once: position moves toward the side with fewer gas particles, decomposition rates of the added species rise, $K_c$ unchanged — the exact 2014 Q31 discrimination (CP 2014 Q31).
  • Report-marking habit: “1 mark for direction of shift + 1 mark for explanation” (ms bank) — direction alone is half the answer.
💡 HKEAA Examiner Watch-Outs:

The killer distractor is “The value of Kc increases”: 56% picked the shift-only option against it (CP 2014 Q31). Removing “the product mixture” is not removing the product — candidates “wrongly used equilibrium shift to explain the practice” in the 2012 industrial item (CP 2012). Species not involved in the reaction: their effect on position is not required by the topic overview — do not speculate in answers.

Topic X (c) Temperature changes

Changes in temperature — the only knob that moves Kc

🎯 Key Learning Goals:
  • Strand (c) verbatim: “a change in temperature results in possible changes in Kc of the system” — heating or cooling changes the value, unlike any concentration change.
  • Predict qualitatively the effect of temperature on the position of equilibrium from the sign of ΔH for the forward reaction: exothermic forward, “Increase in temperature will cause the equilibrium to shift to the left” (ms bank); endothermic forward, heating shifts right.
  • Derive inductively the relation of temperature and the value of Kc from given data sets: if $K_c$ (or percentage conversion) rises with temperature, the forward reaction is endothermic — “Percentage conversion increases as temperature increases… The forward reaction is endothermic” (ms bank); if $K_c$ falls on heating, forward is exothermic.
  • Catalyst discipline: “A catalyst will increase the rate of forward reaction and that of backward reaction to the same extent” and “A catalyst has no effect on the equilibrium position” (ms bank); it only shortens the time to arrive.
  • Keep position / $K_c$ / rate as three separate verdicts in every answer — the two-column habit (position moves … / Kc increases–decreases–stays) is trained in Engine D and the Solver’s temperature bench.
💡 HKEAA Examiner Watch-Outs:

State the direction — do not derive it: “$\ell n K$ = constant − ΔH/RT or log $K$ = constant − ΔH/2.3RT is not expected” (NTF-2018 II-B X (c)); predictions only ever concern a single homogeneous reaction (same ruling). Bare labels lose marks — “just stated that the forward reaction is exothermic but did not give any reasons” (CP 2014 Q13); the catalyst mark needs the “same extent” mechanism wording (CP 2016 Q10). Predicting from the sign of ΔH is qualitative only — no calculation with the temperature law exists at this level.

🚫 Explicitly outside Topic X — say none of these as core (HKEAA depth rulings)

Pressure as an industrial knob → Topic XIII. The Topic X strand list names only concentration and temperature; compression may be reasoned as a concentration effect (all species concentrate at once, CP 2014 Q31 logic), but optimal-pressure treatments and “a more in-depth treatment of the above concepts” live in Topic XIII Industrial Chemistry — and “Details of the contact process are not expected” even there (NTF-2018 II-B X (c)).
Heterogeneous equilibria. “Only a single homogenous reaction will be referred to when predicting changes in concentration and temperature” (NTF-2018 II-B X (c)) — no Kc expressions built on solid–gas systems (no CaCO₃/CaO-type rows, no $K_c$ = [CO₂] shortcuts), no $K_p$ at all.
Deriving the temperature law. The ln / log K versus 1/T equations “is not expected” (NTF-2018 II-B X (c)); the temperature–Kc relation is read from data sets inductively — students state which way Kc moves and why (exo/endo), never compute it.
Species not involved & redox/acid–base depth. “The effect on the position of equilibrium of introducing species not involved in the chemical reaction is not required”, and “Detailed treatment of equilibrium systems involving redox and acid-base reactions are not expected at this level of study” (Topic X overview) — Fe³⁺/SCN⁻ and the dichromate/chromate colour systems appear only as qualitative classroom equilibria.

What “dynamic” means

$$R_{f} = R_{b} \neq 0 \quad \text{(dynamic equilibrium)}$$
🎯 Learning Goals
  • Bank wording, word-perfect: “At dynamic equilibrium, the rate of forward reaction is equal to the rate of backward reaction, and not equals zero” (ms bank).
  • Accepted partner sentence: “reactants are converted to products and products are converted to reactants at equal rate. No net change is observed.” (ms bank) “Dynamic” keeps both arrows alive: equal rates, not absent reaction.
💡 Examiner Tips
  • Omit the “and not equals zero” clause and the definition is half right — in 2016 only about half described it correctly (CP 2016 Q10 / Paper1B).
  • Never let “equal rates” slide into “equal amounts” on the page.
X (a)

The closed-system requirement

$$\text{open vessel} \Rightarrow \text{gas escapes} \Rightarrow R_{b} \text{ starved} \Rightarrow \text{no equilibrium}$$
🎯 Learning Goals
  • Equilibrium needs a closed system: matter enters and leaves nowhere, so $R_{b}$ can climb to meet $R_{f}$; an open vessel loses the gas and the mixture simply runs down.
  • Exam case — ethanamide + aq. NaOH under reflux: “attains equilibrium … heated under reflux” is false: “With the loss of ammonia, an equilibrium state cannot be reached” (CP 2013 Q32 / Paper1A MC).
💡 Examiner Tips
  • Reflux returns condensed solvent but not $\text{NH}_{3}\text{(g)}$ — “under reflux” ≠ “closed to gases”.
  • Answer in two beats: species lost, then consequence — “an equilibrium state cannot be reached”.

Macroscopic constancy

$$[\text{X}],\ \text{colour intensity},\ p,\ \rho = \text{constant at equilibrium}$$
🎯 Learning Goals
  • What we see goes steady: colour intensity, gas pressure, density, every concentration — describe “characteristics of a system existing in dynamic equilibrium”.
  • This constancy is the shadow of microscopic balance: $R_{f} = R_{b} \neq 0$ pins every visible property.
  • The same macroscopic state is reached from either side of the equation (card 5).
💡 Examiner Tips
  • Prove equilibrium with a measured property becoming constant — bank register: “No net change is observed.” (ms bank)
  • Write “constant”, never “equal”, “zero” or “maximum” — completion words.

Equal rates ≠ equal concentrations

$$R_{f} = R_{b} \;\;\text{but in general}\;\; [\text{reactants}] \neq [\text{products}]$$
🎯 Learning Goals
  • The defining equality of (a) is between the two rates — it says nothing about concentration sizes.
  • The composition is the one whose ratio satisfies $K_{c}$ at that temperature — usually unequal; “$[\text{R}] = [\text{P}]$” may coincide but is no criterion.
  • Measured misconception: in 2016 only about half described the term correctly (CP 2016 Q10 / Paper1B) — scripts wrote a concentration equality for the rate equality.
💡 Examiner Tips
  • Proof-read your definition for the word rate: without it there is nothing to award.
  • The invariant is the ratio: $K_{c}$ is “a constant at constant temperature irrespective of changes in concentration” (ms bank).

Approaching equilibrium from both directions

$$\text{H}_{2}\text{(g)} + \text{I}_{2}\text{(g)} \rightleftharpoons 2\text{HI(g)}$$
🎯 Learning Goals
  • Same closed vessel and temperature: start with $\text{H}_{2} + \text{I}_{2}$ ($R_{f}$ starts maximum, $R_{b}$ climbs from zero) or pure HI — the mirror — both routes settle at the identical macroscopic state.
  • Equilibrium is a destination, not a journey detail; sharper still: for one species, its rate of formation equals its rate of decomposition.

🎯 Learning Goals

Four checkpoints into dynamic equilibrium

Clear all four gates, or the steady-looking mixture is lying to you.

Checkpoint 1 · Reversible chemistry

Both changes run under the same conditions — $\text{H}_{2} + \text{I}_{2} \rightleftharpoons 2\text{HI}$, not $\rightarrow$; burning Mg has no second arrow.

Checkpoint 2 · Closed system

Nothing enters or leaves, so every species stays available to collide — else “With the loss of ammonia, an equilibrium state cannot be reached” (CP 2013 Q32 / Paper1A MC).

Checkpoint 3 · Equal non-zero rates

The arrows match speed and keep running: $R_{f} = R_{b} \neq 0$ — “and not equals zero” (ms bank).

Checkpoint 4 · Constant macroscopic properties

Colour intensity, pressure, density, each $[\text{X}]$ hold still — “No net change is observed.” (ms bank) Same state from either side.

Multi-blank Quiz — Dynamic Equilibrium

1. Bank wording: “the rate of forward reaction is equal to the rate of backward reaction, and not equals zero”; conversion continues at equal rate, so “No net change is observed” (ms bank).
2. An open vessel lets a gas escape; equilibrium needs a closed system — ethanamide + NaOH under reflux: “With the loss of ammonia, an equilibrium state cannot be reached” (CP 2013 Q32 / Paper1A MC).
3. The defining equality is between the two rates, not the two concentrations; the composition is fixed by Kc and the sides are generally unequal.
4. 2012 Paper 1A Q27 ($\text{X}_{2} \rightleftharpoons 2\text{X}$, 1 mol $\text{X}_{2}$ in 1 dm³): $[\text{X}_{2}]$ starts at 1.0 mol dm⁻³ and falls to a non-zero plateau; $[\text{X}]$ rises from 0, each mol dm⁻³ of $\text{X}_{2}$ lost yielding 2 mol dm⁻³ of X.
5. Rate–time: the forward rate starts at its maximum; the backward rate starts at zero; the curves merge at a level that is not zero and stay merged.
6. $\text{H}_{2} + \text{I}_{2} \rightleftharpoons 2\text{HI}$: start from reactants or from the product side and the closed vessel reaches the same macroscopic state; HI’s rate of formation then equals its rate of decomposition.
7. $2\text{NO}_{2} \rightleftharpoons \text{N}_{2}\text{O}_{4}$: brown gas ⇌ colourless gas — demo only, no citation; the bank’s $\text{Fe}^{3+} + \text{SCN}^{-} \rightleftharpoons \text{Fe(SCN)}^{2+}$ witness is blood-red (ms bank).
8. “No net change” ≠ “no change”: properties are constant because both directions continue — only net totals stop moving.
9. At equilibrium the reactants remain, conversion stays below 100%; most industrial reactions are reversible (topic overview) — equilibrium is never completion.
10. In 2016 only about half of candidates described dynamic equilibrium correctly (CP 2016 Q10 / Paper1B); “the reaction stopped” is the killer phrase because the bank demands rates “not equals zero” (ms bank).
11. The 2015 item was deleted “due to insufficient discrimination” — its “rate of formation of H2(g)” phrase took different interpretations (CP 2015 Q33 / Paper1A MC); accepted direction wording is “shift to the left hand side / reactant side” (ms bank), 1 mark plus 1 for explanation.

⚠️ Confusion Pairs — Dynamic Equilibrium

“Reaction stops” vs rates equal, non-zero

The steady look hides busy chemistry: “At dynamic equilibrium, the rate of forward reaction is equal to the rate of backward reaction, and not equals zero” (ms bank). Write “stops” and you have drawn the wrong model.

Equal concentrations vs equal rates

The equality that holds is rate = rate; concentrations sit wherever $K_{c}$ puts them — usually unequal. “Equal concentrations” was part of the 2016 half-cohort failure (CP 2016 Q10 / Paper1B).

“No net change” vs “no change”

“No net change is observed” (ms bank) still says both conversions continue; “no change” claims molecular traffic halted. The adjective net does the theoretical work.

Colour constant vs colourless

In the $\text{NO}_{2}/\text{N}_{2}\text{O}_{4}$ tube (demo only, no citation), equilibrium is a fixed brown intensity of a still-brown mixture — not a mixture gone colourless.

Closed vessel vs open vessel

Same chemistry, different verdict: with an escape route for the gas, “With the loss of ammonia, an equilibrium state cannot be reached” (CP 2013 Q32 / Paper1A MC) — the closed vessel is part of the definition.

“Reversible” vs “the reverse is happening now”

Reversible labels the equation’s capability; dynamic equilibrium asserts the reverse reaction is running at equal rate now. A reversible reaction in an open vessel never gets there.

“Rate of formation of H₂” — ambiguity

A lone “rate of formation” can read as net or forward-only — the HKEAA deleted its 2015 item “due to insufficient discrimination” when candidates read “the term ‘rate of formation of H2(g)’” with different interpretations (CP 2015 Q33 / Paper1A MC). Say: formation of X equals decomposition of X.

The units ladder — four gates from equation to answer

Every expression-and-units mark walks four gates; miss one and the units invert, a power vanishes, or a solid sneaks in.

Gate 1 · Balanced equation & Δn

Confirm the equation balances; compute Δn = Σν(products) − Σν(reactants) from the coefficients: 2SO₂ + O₂ ⇌ 2SO₃ gives 2 − 3 = −1; H₂ + I₂ ⇌ 2HI gives 2 − 2 = 0. Δn is the net power of the unit.

Gate 2 · Powers & roster

Products on top, reactants below, each coefficient becomes an exponent: [SO₃]² above, [SO₂]² and [O₂]¹ below. (g) and (aq) enter; (s) and pure (l) never appear — Topic X stays homogeneous (NTF-2018 II-B X (c)).

Gate 3 · Substitute the units

Replace every bracket by mol dm⁻³ and rebuild the fraction with the real exponents: for 2SO₂ + O₂ ⇌ 2SO₃, unit = (mol dm⁻³)² / ((mol dm⁻³)²(mol dm⁻³).

Gate 4 · Simplify & sign-check

Collapse to molΔn dm−3Δn: −1 → mol⁻¹ dm³; −2 → mol⁻² dm⁶; +1 → mol dm⁻³; +2 → mol² dm⁻⁶; 0 → “no units”. Bottom-heavy cannot end in plain mol dm⁻³.

Multi-blank Quiz — Kc Expressions & Units

1. For aA + bB ⇌ cC + dD, Kc puts the products over the reactants, each raised to its balancing coefficient; a square bracket means the equilibrium concentration in mol dm⁻³.
2. The bank notes a “Correct expression for Kc” is worth 1 mark even if the numbers are wrong (ms bank) — write the expression before any ICE arithmetic.
3. For PCl₅(g) ⇌ PCl₃(g) + Cl₂(g): Kc = [PCl₃][Cl₂] / [PCl₅]; Δn = +1, so the units are mol dm⁻³.
4. In N₂(g) + 3H₂(g) ⇌ 2NH₃(g), Kc = [NH₃]² / ([N₂][H₂]³) — the power is the coefficient — and Δn = 2 − 4 = −2 gives units of mol⁻² dm⁶.
5. Pure solids and pure liquids stay out because their concentration is constant (absorbed into Kc); Topic X assesses only a single homogeneous reaction (NTF-2018 II-B X (c)) — while aqueous ions such as SCN⁻(aq) must be kept in.
6. In CH₃COOH + C₂H₅OH ⇌ CH₃COOC₂H₅ + H₂O the water is in the expression; as a mere solvent it would be omitted. Conc. H₂SO₄ shifts the position because it is “a dehydrating agent / removes water from the product side” (ms bank).
7. The 2014 item 56% missed: decreasing the volume of 2SO₂(g) + O₂(g) ⇌ 2SO₃(g) at constant temperature raises the concentration of all species, the position shifts, but the distractor “The value of Kc increases” is false — only a change in temperature alters Kc (CP 2014 Q31).
8. Q has the same expression shape as Kc but uses current concentrations; the bank’s deduction runs “reaction quotient > Kc, so backward reaction rate is greater than the forward reaction rate” (ms bank).
9. Substituting mol dm⁻³ into Kc = [SO₃]² / ([SO₂]²[O₂]) gives unit molΔn dm−3Δn with Δn = −1, i.e. mol⁻¹ dm³; CP’s recorded “failed to give a correct unit for the answer” (CP 2014 Q13).
10. A very large Kc means the mixture is mostly products; it does not mean the reaction is fast — Kc reports position, not rate, and even then the two rates stay equal and not zero (ms bank).
11. If H₂(g) + I₂(g) ⇌ 2HI(g) has Kc = 4.0, then 2HI(g) ⇌ H₂(g) + I₂(g) has Kc = 1/4.0 = 0.25 and 2H₂(g) + 2I₂(g) ⇌ 4HI(g) has Kc = 4.0² = 16.0; all three are unitless because every form keeps Δn = 0.

⚠️ Confusion Pairs — Kc Expressions & Units

Solids out — aqueous ions in (keep them!)

The exclusion is about phase. [CaCO₃(s)] or [C(s)] never belongs (constant concentration), and Topic X stays homogeneous anyway (NTF-2018 II-B X (c)). But Fe³⁺(aq), SCN⁻(aq) change concentration — dropping them loses the mark.

Products/reactants flip — you built 1/Kc

Kc is products over reactants. [SO₂]²[O₂]/[SO₃]² is not “roughly the other way round” — it is the reverse reaction’s constant, the reciprocal (card 7). Check the line against the arrow.

Coefficients vs powers — the forgotten square

Powers come from coefficients: [SO₃]² needs the 2; [H₂]³ needs the 3. Plain [SO₃]/([SO₂][O₂]) silently changes the law and forfeits the standalone “Correct expression for Kc” mark (ms bank).

mol dm⁻³ vs mol⁻¹ dm³ — the units inversion

Δn’s sign decides: −1 → mol⁻¹ dm³ (SO₃, FeSCN); −2 → mol⁻² dm⁶ (NH₃); +1 → mol dm⁻³ (PCl₅). The cm³-vs-dm³ blunder is Engine C’s beast; here guard the sign of Δn.

“Kc changes when we add reactant”

No — the position moves; “Kc depends only on temperature — independent of concentration (ALLOW independent of pressure)” (master summary §5.2). The 2014 distractor “The value of Kc increases” collected most of the scripts (CP 2014 Q31).

Initial vs equilibrium concentrations

Brackets in Kc mean the ICE final line over volume. Start-of-experiment numbers compute Q — useful (card 8), but not Kc and no Kc mark.

Big Kc ≠ fast reaction

Magnitude says where the mixture sits, not how quickly — a huge-Kc equilibrium can crawl without a catalyst; a 2015 item was deleted over the “rate of formation of H2(g)” ambiguity (CP 2015 Q33).

  • Strand (b) requires students to "perform practical work on the determination of Kc"; the syllabus activity names the "esterification of alkanol and alkanoic acid".

The ICE highway — four gates to Kc

Every strand-(b) calculation runs the same road — find the gate you keep crashing at.

Gate 1 · Moles into the I row

Every species enters in moles — including anything added later, at its full amount (CP 2013). Zero only where none was present.

Gate 2 · The C row: −ax / +cx

One x anchored to the coefficients; direction from the stem or Q versus Kc (ms bank). Signs flip together, never column by column.

Gate 3 · E row, then ÷V

Add rows in moles, then divide by the volume in dm³ (250 cm³ = 0.250 dm³). The famous "50 cm3 instead of 50 dm3" crash happened here (CP 2014 Q13).

Gate 4 · Substitute, simplify, units

Powers = coefficients; square before combining; units from (mol dm⁻³)^Δn — or the honest "no units" when Δn = 0.

⚠️ Exam Traps — ICE Tables & Kc Arithmetic

Moles vs concentration confusion

Kc eats mol dm⁻³, not moles — an E-row mole substitution shifts the answer by a power of V: candidates "failed to recognise that the volume of the reaction container is 10.0 dm3, and were unable to arrive at the correct answer for the reaction quotient" (CP 2013).

Volume plugged raw in cm³

250 cm³ is 0.250 dm³, never "250" in a denominator: candidates "wrongly regarded the total volume of the container as 50 cm3 instead of 50 dm3" (CP 2014 Q13). A raw plug shrinks every bracket 1000-fold; squared terms wreck Kc by up to 10⁶.

Change row ignoring the coefficients

For aA ⇌ cC the entries are −ax and +cx; in 2SO₂ + O₂ ⇌ 2SO₃ that is −2x, −x, +2x — a blanket −x breaks the grid. Ratios come from the balanced equation, signs from the direction taken (Q versus Kc).

The ADDED species never enters the I row

Extra CO₂ enters at its added amount, not zero — the shift cannot bury it: "the final [CO2(g)] cannot be lower than the initial [CO2(g)]" (CP 2013). An E cell below the addition is physically impossible.

Square-rooting when Δn ≠ 0

x²/(1−x)² = 4 is rootable because both sides are perfect squares; x²/(c−x) = Kc is not — rooting it invents arithmetic. CP logged the algebra deaths: "unable to solve the quadratic equation involved" (CP 2013) and "failed to correctly solve the corresponding equations" (CP 2016 Q10(b)). Expand, solve, reject the negative-mole root.

Kc without units — or with the wrong ones

Units are derived: (mol dm⁻³)^Δn gives mol dm⁻³ for PCl₅, mol⁻¹ dm³ for 2SO₂ + O₂ ⇌ 2SO₃, nothing for H₂ + I₂ ⇌ 2HI. "failed to give a correct unit for the answer" (CP 2014 Q13); "Incorrect units were commonly presented after calculation" (CP 2012).

Titrating a mixture that never stopped reacting

The determination flask must be quenched — cooled/diluted fast — before the NaOH titration; otherwise the mixture keeps reacting through the analysis, no equilibrium state is measured, and the ester figure reads too low. Subtract the concentrated-H₂SO₄ blank too, or catalyst acid masquerades as unreacted ethanoic acid (ms bank).

Le Chatelier: Shifts, Kc & Temperature

Two knobs only: concentration moves the position but never Kc, temperature moves both, and a catalyst is a clock. It trains the shift argument, the Q check behind it, and the wording that banks the marks.

Le Chatelier’s principle — partial counteraction

$$\text{stress} \;\rightarrow\; \text{response PARTIALLY counteracts it} \;\rightarrow\; Q \;\rightarrow\; K_{c}$$
🎯 Learning Goals
  • The system adjusts to partially counteract the stress, stopping when $Q$ again equals the $K_{c}$ fixed at that temperature.
  • Strand (c) wording: “changes in concentration result in the adjustment of the system without changing the value of Kc” versus “a change in temperature results in possible changes in Kc of the system”.
  • Activity wording: “exploring how Le Chatelier’s Principle can be used for predicting the shift in equilibrium position and recognising its limitations.”
💡 Examiner Tips
  • Limitation: direction only, never extent — Engine B’s Kc arithmetic gives the size of the change.
  • “Partial”: an added species is still above its old value at the new equilibrium — eased, not erased.
X (c)

Concentration ↑ — adding a reactant or a product

$$\text{H}_{2}\text{(g)} + \text{I}_{2}\text{(g)} \rightleftharpoons 2\text{HI}\text{(g)} \qquad K_{c} = \frac{[\text{HI}]^{2} }{[\text{H}_{2}][\text{I}_{2}]}$$
🎯 Learning Goals
  • Add a reactant or product and the position shifts away from the addition, toward the side that consumes it (add HI above → left).
  • Temperature is unchanged, so the new equilibrium still satisfies the same Kc — only the concentrations are re-divided.
  • Register: “The equilibrium position will shift to the left hand side / reactant side” (ms bank) — name the side, then explain it.
💡 Examiner Tips
  • Two marks sit in one sentence: “1 mark for direction of shift + 1 mark for explanation” (ms bank).
  • Counteract is not cancel, and no concentration change moves Kc: it is “independent of concentration” (master summary §5.2).

Removal & dilution — the shift runs toward the loss

$$\text{CH}_{3}\text{COOH} + \text{C}_{2}\text{H}_{5}\text{OH} \rightleftharpoons \text{CH}_{3}\text{COOC}_{2}\text{H}_{5} + \text{H}_{2}\text{O} \qquad K_{c} = \frac{[\text{ester}][\text{H}_{2}\text{O}]}{[\text{acid}][\text{alcohol}]}$$
🎯 Learning Goals
  • Remove something and the position shifts toward what was removed, Kc unchanged. Esterification is the clean case: concentrated H₂SO₄ is a “dehydrating agent / removes water from the product side” (ms bank).
  • Water rule, kept exactly: in homogeneous esterification-type equilibria water appears in the expression; as solvent or pure liquid it is omitted, its constant concentration absorbed into Kc.
💡 Examiner Tips
  • The argument works only for the species actually taken out: candidates “confused the two ideas 'removal of product mixture' with 'removal of product (ammonia)', and hence they wrongly used equilibrium shift to explain the practice” (CP 2012 / Paper2 industrial context).
  • Nothing removed from the mixture, nothing to explain.

Doing it with Q — the formal (b)→(c) bridge

$$K_{c} = 4.0;\ [\text{HI}] = 0.40,\ [\text{H}_{2}] = [\text{I}_{2}] = 0.10 \qquad Q = \frac{(0.40)^{2} }{(0.10)(0.10)} = \frac{0.16}{0.010} = 16 > K_{c}$$
🎯 Learning Goals
  • After a concentration change compute $Q$ from the instantaneous concentrations: $Q > K_{c}$ ⇒ net backward; $Q < K_{c}$ ⇒ net forward; $Q = K_{c}$ ⇒ no net change.
  • Worked: HI injected to 0.40 mol dm⁻³ at [H₂] = [I₂] = 0.10 mol dm⁻³ gives Q = 16 against Kc = 4.0, so HI decomposes net until Q falls back to 4.0.
  • Bank sentence: “reaction quotient > Kc, so backward reaction rate is greater than the forward reaction rate” (ms bank); the route is required (NTF-2018 II-B X (c)).
💡 Examiner Tips
  • Divide by the volume in dm³ first, then compare Q with Kc and name the direction.
  • Candidates “failed to recognise that the volume of the reaction container is 10.0 dm3” (CP 2013 / Paper1B context).

Compression & dilution of ALL species — the 2014 Q31 logic

$$V \downarrow \;\Rightarrow\; [\text{SO}_{3}]^{2}\times 4,\quad [\text{SO}_{2}]^{2}[\text{O}_{2}]\times 8 \;\Rightarrow\; Q' = \frac{Q}{2} < K_{c}$$
🎯 Learning Goals
  • Volume ↓ at constant T raises every concentration, and the sides are raised to different powers (2 against 3 particles), so Q jumps unevenly: $Q \ne K_{c}$.
  • The position moves toward fewer gaseous particles; dilution (V ↑) favours more (PCl₅ ⇌ PCl₃ + Cl₂ shifts right). Kc unchanged, and H₂ + I₂ ⇌ 2HI (equal both sides) does not shift.

Temperature ↑ — the knob that rewrites Kc

$$\text{N}_{2}\text{(g)} + 3\text{H}_{2}\text{(g)} \rightleftharpoons 2\text{NH}_{3}\text{(g)} \quad \Delta H < 0 \qquad T \uparrow \;\Rightarrow\; \text{shift left},\ K_{c} \downarrow$$
🎯 Learning Goals
  • Heating is a heat stress: the system responds in the endothermic direction, absorbing some of the added heat.
  • The constant moves too: forward exothermic ($\Delta H < 0$) ⇒ Kc falls; forward endothermic ($\Delta H > 0$) ⇒ Kc rises — strand (c): “a change in temperature results in possible changes in Kc of the system”.
💡 Examiner Tips
  • Bank model: “The reaction is exothermic. Increase in temperature will cause the equilibrium to shift to the left” (ms bank) — sign first, then direction.
  • Never pair a shift with a Kc change unless temperature changed (master summary §5.2).
X (c)

Temperature ↓ — the mirror, drilled in two columns

$$T \downarrow \;\Rightarrow\; \text{shift exothermic way} \qquad \begin{cases} \Delta H < 0\ (\text{fwd exo}): & K_{c} \uparrow \\ \Delta H > 0\ (\text{fwd endo}): & K_{c} \downarrow \end{cases}$$
🎯 Learning Goals
  • Cooling removes heat, so the system shifts in the exothermic direction; Kc moves opposite to the heating answer for the same reaction.
  • Read ΔH for the forward reaction as written — the “able” line is to “predict qualitatively the effect of temperature on the position of equilibrium from the sign of ΔH for the forward reaction”.
  • Habit: two columns — position (left / right / no change) and Kc (increases / decreases / unchanged), never one blended sentence.
💡 Examiner Tips
  • A leftward shift does not imply a falling Kc: exothermic forward reaction plus cooling gives shift right and Kc up.
  • Keep “T ↑ and Kc ↓ → forward reaction exothermic” (master summary §5.2), inverted for cooling.

Reading T–Kc data inductively — the numbers give ΔH

$$\begin{array}{c|cc} T / \text{K} & 300 & 500 \\ \hline K_{c} & 2.5 & 0.04 \end{array} \qquad T \uparrow,\ K_{c} \downarrow \;\Rightarrow\; \text{forward exothermic}$$
🎯 Learning Goals
  • The outcome is inductive, not algebraic: “derive inductively the relation of temperature and the value of Kc from given data sets”.
  • Kc or percentage conversion rising with temperature ⇒ forward reaction endothermic: “Percentage conversion increases as temperature increases… The forward reaction is endothermic” (ms bank). Falling ⇒ exothermic.
  • Hard cap: no ln K = constant − ΔH/RT, no log K against 1/T — “not expected” (NTF-2018 II-B X (c)).
💡 Examiner Tips
  • The reason must cite the data: an answer “just stated that the forward reaction is exothermic but did not give any reasons” (CP 2014 Q13).
  • Answer shape: observation (Kc or % conversion trend) → direction of shift → conclusion about ΔH.
X (a)

Catalyst — it buys time, not position

$$\text{catalyst} \;\Rightarrow\; v(\text{forward}) \uparrow \ \text{and} \ v(\text{backward}) \uparrow \ \text{equally} \;\Rightarrow\; Q = K_{c} \ \text{still}$$
🎯 Learning Goals
  • Bank wording, verbatim: “A catalyst will increase the rate of forward reaction and that of backward reaction to the same extent” and “A catalyst has no effect on the equilibrium position” (ms bank).
  • Both rates are multiplied by the same factor, so equality survives at the same composition: the mixture ends where it would have done, sooner.
  • Contrast drill on heating: the claim that heating increases the yield of every reversible reaction is false, the claim that it shortens the time to attain equilibrium is true → 1st false, 2nd true.
💡 Examiner Tips
  • The mark is on the explanation: candidates “only stated that adding a suitable catalyst would not increase the number of moles of SO3(g) obtained without giving a proper explanation” (CP 2016 Q10(c)(ii)).
  • Catalysis is not “providing more H⁺” (CP 2013 Q1 / Paper2 Section A context), and it never adds yield.

Three separate questions — position, Kc, rate

$$\text{position? left / right} \qquad K_{c}\text{? temperature only} \qquad \text{rate? } c,\ T,\ \text{catalyst}$$
🎯 Learning Goals
  • Three questions, three vocabularies. Position: left / right / no change (justified by Q against Kc). Kc: temperature only. Rate: concentration, temperature, catalyst.
  • Each sentence must name the question it answers.
  • Wording is dangerous: the MC on the rate of formation of H₂(g) for CO(g) + H₂O(g) ⇌ CO₂(g) + H₂(g) was deleted “due to insufficient discrimination… different interpretations of the term 'rate of formation of H2(g)'” (CP 2015 Q33).

  • If one part asks both, write two labelled sentences.

⚠️ Confusion Pairs — Shifts, Kc & Temperature

Catalyst shifts the position

Both rates rise “to the same extent”, so “A catalyst has no effect on the equilibrium position” (ms bank) — only time is saved. The 2016 mark needed the mechanism, not a claim about moles of SO₃ (CP 2016 Q10(c)(ii)).

“Equilibrium shifts right so Kc increases”

A concentration stress is “the adjustment of the system without changing the value of Kc”; Kc depends only on temperature (master summary §5.2). Give the two verdicts separately.

“Yield always increases when the shift is right”

A rightward shift raises the amount formed from what is now in the flask, not necessarily the percentage conversion of the original charge: adding reactant leaves part unconverted. State the direction; let Kc decide how much.

Answering a rate question with a position

A shift sentence never answers a “rate of formation” question — rate is collisions per unit volume per unit time. The ambiguity got a 2015 MC deleted (CP 2015 Q33).

“Temperature changes only the forward rate”

Heating speeds both directions, unequally: the endothermic one gains more, so the position moves that way and Kc is rewritten. Name the endothermic direction; never say the other one “stops”.

“Compression raises Kc” — the 2014 distractor

Compression lifts all concentrations, which is why Q jumps and the position moves toward fewer gaseous particles — and why over half the cohort accepted “the value of Kc increases” (CP 2014 Q31). H₂ + I₂ ⇌ 2HI does not shift at all.

Removing “product mixture” vs the product

The argument fires only for a species in the expression: candidates “confused the two ideas 'removal of product mixture' with 'removal of product (ammonia)'” and wrongly used a shift to explain it (CP 2012 / Paper2 industrial context) — naming the removed species is the mark.

The bench echoes the substitution and derives units as $(\text{mol dm}^{-3})^{\Delta n}$: the HI case returns no units because $\Delta n = 0$; SO₃ formation gives mol⁻¹ dm³. “The unit must be correct” and “Correct expression for Kc = 1 mark even if numbers wrong” (ms bank; CP 2014 Q13).

Bench 2

ICE chain — initial moles + one equilibrium amount → Kc

Defaults reproduce Engine C’s worked case: 0.40 mol PCl₅ in 2.0 dm³, 0.16 mol Cl₂ at equilibrium → $x = 0.16$, ICE moles 0.24 / 0.16 / 0.16 → concentrations 0.12 / 0.08 / 0.08 mol dm⁻³ → $K_c = 0.08 \times 0.08 \div 0.12 = 0.0533$ mol dm⁻³ — “Step marks (*) for ICE table calculations” (ms bank). Watch the volume: 50 cm³ ≠ 50 dm³ (CP 2014 Q13).

Bench 3

Direction predictor — reaction quotient vs Kc

$Q$ uses current concentrations in the same expression as $K_c$. The verdict copies the bank’s register: “reaction quotient > Kc, so backward reaction rate is greater than the forward reaction rate” (ms bank); using $Q$ to deduce the effect of a concentration change is explicitly expected (NTF-2018 II-B X (c)). Try Engine D’s worked stress: [HI] jumped to 0.40 with [H₂] = [I₂] = 0.10 → Q = 16 > Kc = 4 → net backward.

Bench 4

Temperature effect — two-column answer builder

One card, two columns: position (shifts in the endothermic direction on heating; exothermic on cooling) and Kc (rises when the endothermic direction is favoured, falls otherwise) — the only change that moves $K_c$ (ms bank). Every sentence carries its reason: bare “it is exothermic” was rejected (CP 2014 Q13); no ln K / log K working is needed or allowed (NTF-2018 II-B X (c)).

Why this matters — the exam chain

A Paper 1B equilibrium question runs the benches in one line: the stem gives a mixture and a volume → Bench 2 converts it through ICE moles → equilibrium concentrations → Bench 1 hands you $K_c$ with its units (the mark line you are actually paid on) → a follow-up “extra X(g) is added, predict what happens” goes through Bench 3 ($Q$ vs $K_c$, then the bank’s direction wording) → the final part heats the vessel and Bench 4 writes the two-column verdict in one sentence each. General comments have flagged “calculations on chemical equilibrium” for this exact chain (CP 2012 general comments; CP 2013 general comments) — practise it until the ordering is boring.

1 · Definitions to state word-perfect (Engines A–D)▾
TermExam wording
Reversible reactionA reaction in which products react to re-form reactants under the same conditions; written with ⇌ and able to reach equilibrium in a closed system (e.g. N₂ + 3H₂ ⇌ 2NH₃, esterification).
Irreversible reactionA reaction that proceeds to completion in the given direction — products do not re-form reactants to any appreciable extent (e.g. burning magnesium).
Dynamic equilibriumIn a closed system: the rate of the forward reaction equals the rate of the backward reaction, and not equals zero; reactants and products interconvert at equal rate, so no net change is observed.
Closed systemOne that exchanges no matter with the surroundings — no species escapes, so equilibrium can establish (gas escaping under reflux prevents it: “With the loss of ammonia, an equilibrium state cannot be reached”).
Homogeneous equilibriumAn equilibrium in which all species are in the same phase; Topic X assessment restricts to a single homogeneous reaction.
Equilibrium constant KcThe ratio of products of equilibrium concentrations (each raised to its balancing coefficient, products over reactants); constant at constant temperature irrespective of concentration changes.
Reaction quotient QThe same expression as Kc but evaluated with current (not necessarily equilibrium) concentrations; Q > Kc → net backward, Q < Kc → net forward, Q = Kc → at equilibrium.
Equilibrium positionThe relative amounts of reactants and products at equilibrium (“shift to the left hand side / reactant side” is HKEAA register for its movement).
Percentage conversion / yieldFraction of reactants converted to product at equilibrium (× 100%); reading “percentage conversion increases as temperature increases” inductively identifies the endothermic direction.
Degree of dissociationFraction of the original molecules that have split at equilibrium (α), used for PCl₅-type equilibria: 1 mol → α mol each product + (1 − α) mol left.
Le Chatelier’s principleIf a system at equilibrium is stressed, the position adjusts to partially counteract the stress (partially, never fully; direction only — the limitation). Kc changes only when temperature changes.
Catalyst (equilibrium role)Increases the rate of forward reaction and that of backward reaction to the same extent; no effect on the equilibrium position or on Kc — equilibrium is attained faster.

7 · Misconceptions — wrong idea → exam-true correction → source▾
The wrong ideaExam-true correctionSource
“At equilibrium the reaction has stopped.”Forward and backward reactions continue at equal, non-zero rates — “No net change is observed”, nothing more.ms bank; CP 2016 Q10
“Equilibrium means reactants and products are in equal amounts.”The ratio [products]/[reactants]-weighted is fixed by Kc — equal only by coincidence when Kc ≈ 1.syllabus (b); CP 2016 Q10(a) context
“A catalyst makes more product at equilibrium.”It increases both rates “to the same extent”; amount of SO₃ etc. is unchanged — only arrival is faster.ms bank; CP 2016 Q10(c)(ii)
“Adding reactant increases Kc (reaction ‘wants’ to use it up).”Q drops below the UNCHANGED Kc; the position adjusts until Q = Kc again. Constants don’t take preferences.syllabus (c); ms bank Q line
“Compressing the vessel increases Kc.”Compression raises every concentration and moves the position toward fewer gas particles; Kc unchanged — that distractor fooled 56%.CP 2014 Q31
“Heating shifts right (or left) — always.”Heating favours the ENDOTHERMIC direction only; direction needs the sign of ΔH for the forward reaction.syllabus (c); ms bank exo line
“A big Kc means a fast reaction.”Kc grades position (how far), rate is kinetics (how soon) — diamond’s formation has a huge Kc at room T and never happens visibly.Engine B card 6; Topic IX split
