The Iodine Clock Reaction

Peroxodisulfate–iodide clock: measure initial rates with the blue-black flash, deduce orders, k and Ea.

Myth

The “2” in front of 2I⁻ means the reaction is second order in iodide.

Fact

Orders are EXPERIMENTAL, never from coefficients. Here rate = k[S₂O₈²⁻][I⁻] — first order in each, total order 2.

Myth

Heating speeds the clock up by lowering the activation energy.

Fact

Ea is FIXED. Heating raises the proportion of collisions with E ≥ Ea, so k = A·e^(−Ea/RT) rises — Ea only falls when a catalyst opens a new route.

Myth

The blue-black flash means the peroxodisulfate has all reacted.

Fact

It marks the instant the tiny fixed dose of S₂O₃²⁻ is used up — only a small % of the main reactants have gone, so t measures the initial rate.
